Dear Dr. Degenfellner,

Thank you for your careful independent rerun of our eBay vaporizer analysis and for
taking the time to report what you could and could not reproduce. Your message
prompted a full version-of-record audit of the repository, and it surfaced a real
defect on our side that I want to acknowledge directly.

On the bootstrap, difference-in-differences, and CITS sensitivity analyses: you were
correct that the implementing code was not reachable from the repository's default
branch. The code did exist — it was written during the revision and produced exactly
the numbers reported in the article — but it had been left on an unmerged working
branch and was never carried into the public repository. That is our
repository-organization error, not a failure of your rerun. The script
(revision_sensitivity_analysis.py) and its outputs are now included in the open
audit pull request on the repository (still under review, not yet merged), together
with a traceability matrix mapping the principal published results to the code
that produces them.

On your specific question about the Kendall test: the article's "ordered regulatory
phases" is implemented as Kendall's tau-b (the scipy default) of price against an
ordinal phase index coded 1-5. The five phases are delimited by left-exclusive
boundaries at 2022-04-05, 2023-10-05, 2024-02-07, and 2026-01-01; the first phase
is empty because the Terapeak window begins March 2023, so in practice sales are
distributed over phases 2-5. This is distinct from the Spearman test, which uses the
individual sale dates. This specification was under-documented in the repository and
is now stated explicitly in the README; thank you for pressing on it.

I should be transparent about residual limits rather than overstate the outcome:
the audit environment uses slightly different package versions than those stated in
the paper (all principal published results still reproduce identically), and the ScienceDirect
PDF could not be diffed programmatically against the PMC full text we used as the
reference. The DiD and CITS models are, as the article states, sensitivity analyses
whose causal assumptions are only partly met.

Thank you also for the kind invitation regarding the Journal of Reproducible Health
Sciences. I am genuinely interested in computational verification as a publication
model — an independently executed rerun with a preserved environment record is, I
think, the most honest form of replication. I would like to keep the possibility of
a submission open without committing to a timeline just yet.

To that end, would you be willing to share the run log and software environment
(Python and package versions) from your attempted reproduction? If we document your
rerun alongside ours, it would make the cross-environment evidence much stronger.

With appreciation for the rigor of your check,
